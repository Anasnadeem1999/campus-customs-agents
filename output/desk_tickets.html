Customer's note
“Needs a tee in size S.”

Expected

Boss calls Inventory first.

On its face this is one question — can we put a size-S tee in a customer's hand? Until that is answered there is nothing to spend money on and nothing to tell the customer, so every other specialist would be answering a question nobody has asked yet. Calling Accounting first would mean asking “can we afford it?” before knowing what it is or whether it is even needed; the shelf might simply have one.

It is also the cheapest call on the board. One tool, one answer, and it either closes the ticket outright or reframes it — which is exactly what we expect here.

The delegations we expect, in order

#Who asks whomThe questionWhy this colleague
1 Boss → Inventory Can we fill one CC-TEE-WHITE in S, and if not, who can reprint it and when? Stock by size, shortfalls and vendor lead times are Inventory's and nobody else's.
2 Boss → Accounting Invoice 501 is blocking the only apparel vendor. What is owed, can the shop pay it, and will you raise the approval? Only once Inventory reports the block does this become a money question. Cash and approvals are Accounting's.
3 Boss → Customer Service Draft Tauhid a reply: S is empty, a reprint is pending approval, ETA 2026-09-05, and these sizes are on the shelf today. A real customer is waiting on an answer. Nobody else writes what a customer reads.
Facilities is not called, and that is deliberate.

This ticket has no lease, no rent and no premises question. Asking Facilities would cost a model call to be told, correctly, that it has nothing to add. Three of the four specialists are enough here; the fourth would be padding.

Ticket 102 — Rent due

rent_notice
From
Elm City Properties (the landlord)
Ticket links to
lease 1
The claim
“Email: shop rent due in 2 days.”

Expected

Boss calls Facilities first.

The lease is Facilities' and nobody else's, and this ticket is a claim rather than a fact: a landlord's email asserting what is owed. The first move is to check it against the lease the shop actually signed, and Facilities is the one that reads leases.

Calling Accounting first would skip that check and go straight to “can we afford $2,400?” — taking the landlord's figure on trust. A shop that pays what the invoice says without opening the contract is a shop that quietly overpays for years.

The delegations we expect, in order

#Who asks whomThe questionWhy this colleague
1 Boss → Facilities Does the lease agree with this notice — landlord, amount, due date? Leases and the landlord relationship are Facilities' remit.
2 Facilities → Accounting The lease confirms $2,400 due 2026-09-02. Does that fit, and will you raise the approval? Specialist to specialist, not back through the Boss. Facilities has a verified figure and one follow-on question; routing it via the Boss would add a turn and change nothing. This is the delegation that shows the team is genuinely fully connected rather than a hub and spokes.
Inventory and Customer Service are both not called.
  • Inventory — no stock, no SKU, no size. Nothing to say.
  • Customer Service — the landlord is not a customer, and the shop rules forbid replying to them at all. Drafting a message here would be work that must never be sent.

Two specialists on this ticket, not four. It is the simplest of the three and the plan should look it.

The one ticket that should actually finish. awaiting_approval on the first pass, then resolved once a human signs and the payment goes through. Nothing here depends on a vendor, a lead time or a customer's patience — only on the owner saying yes.

Watch for the failure mode: confirming the rent is genuinely owed and stopping there. That leaves the landlord unpaid with the ticket closed, which is worse than leaving it open, because nobody is looking for it any more.

This is the call we most expect a weaker plan to get wrong. The ticket is typed price_override and reads like a pricing question, which argues for Accounting. We expect Inventory, because a discount on units the shop cannot supply is not a deal — only 8 of the 20 are on the shelf.

The shortfall is also the input Accounting needs: the real cost of this deal is not the discount, it is the $264 of stock that must be bought before a single unit ships. Ask Accounting first and it prices 20 hoodies the shop does not have.

What did not move the balance, and why
